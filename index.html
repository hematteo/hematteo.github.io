Sparse Readout Prism

Explaining Logit-Lens Scores in Features Instead of Tokens

Matteo He, William F. Shen, Xinchi Qiu, Nicholas D. Lane

How do a lens’s fitting choices affect what it reports?

I found that English- and Chinese-fitted Jacobian lenses could report different languages for identical Qwen3.5-9B hidden states. Sparse Readout Prism instead decomposes lens scores through shared features of the model’s output weights, with explicit residuals and local readout-side ablations.

Paper Code Dictionaries BibTeX
Method & scope

The method fits sparse autoencoders directly to LM-head weights and decomposes selected logits and margins into signed feature contributions. Across six readouts without softcaps, it improved logit-difference reconstruction coverage by 8.9–17.3 percentage points over the strongest of six geometric baselines. Coverage requires the correct sign and less than 50% relative error. Reconstruction, replacement, sign, residual, and confound checks bound what the explanation establishes.

Learning to Read Out

Unembedding Dynamics in Language Model Pretraining

Matteo He, William F. Shen, Alex Iacob, Andrej Jovanovic, Xinchi Qiu, Nicholas D. Lane

When is information available before the model’s own readout expresses it?

On Pythia-6.9B, I found subject–verb agreement linearly decodable from hidden states several hundred steps before native-readout expression; gaps persisted at convergence on harder relative-clause contrasts. This exposes a measurement confound in output-based capability timelines.

Code Guided notebooks BibTeX
Method & scope

I extract output-weight snapshots over pretraining, fit trajectory crosscoders to their development, and combine readout swaps, linear probes, and feature ablations. On fixed Pythia-1B step-1000 hidden states, removing eight localized readout features from the reconstructed terminal readout reduced held-out agreement accuracy from 90.0% to 48.7%; matched controls stayed at 90.0%. In single-seed 31M-parameter control runs, each 4× increase in output-readout learning rate halved the reorganization step; final validation losses agreed within 0.06 nats.

The public release includes reusable analysis components, CPU-only examples, guided notebooks, and an experiment-to-figure manifest. The manuscript remains under review.

2024Honours dissertation · St Andrews

Automating the Interpretability of Attention Heads in LLMs

Can language models generate and test explanations of attention-head behavior?

I built a GPT-2 interpretability pipeline using an LLM explainer and simulator to propose and evaluate functional explanations. The project improved distribution-based evaluation metrics by 25% and reduced token errors from 366 to 157 across the dissertation’s evaluation.

Approach & evaluation

The dissertation paired GPT-4 as an explainer with GPT-3.5 as a simulator, then compared predicted and observed attention through distributional and token-level errors. It generated high-fidelity explanations across at least ten syntactic attention heads. These results refer to the dissertation’s experimental setup.
